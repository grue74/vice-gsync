6581 filter sliders: what moves where

Each plot shows the reSIDfp 6581 cutoff frequency against the FC register (0 to 2047) while one slider changes and the rest stay at your "6581R4 (3 tunes, v2)" values, with the knots at 0. The cutoff scale is logarithmic, so equal heights mean equal musical intervals.

Sliders that leave the cutoff curve where it is

SliderWhat it changesWhy the curve stays
Resonance QHeight of the resonance peak at every resonance value, 15 included.It speeds one integrator up and slows the other down by the same factor. The cutoff depends on their product, the Q on their ratio.
Resonance strengthHow much each resonance register step adds. No effect at resonance 15 on the 6581.It scales the feedback ladder, which sets Q, not the integrator speed.
Filter caps C1/C2, oppositeResonance, a little (C2 up and C1 down gives more).The cutoff follows C1 × C2, so opposite changes cancel. Moving both the same way does move the curve, like Filter range.
Wave offsetThe DC level of the voices going into the filter: distortion, and how loud volume-register clicks and digis are.It acts on the signal, not on the cutoff control voltage.
Combined wavesHow the mixed waveforms (e.g. $51, $61) sound.Waveform generator only.

A tuning order that works

  1. Snake resistor for the lowest cutoff: listen to a closed filter, low FC.
  2. Filter range to bring the whole curve up or down together.
  3. Filter curve for where the filter starts to open as FC rises.
  4. Op-amp gain for how fast it opens through the middle.
  5. DAC kink if sweeps jump audibly at FC 512 or 1024.
  6. Knots for whatever bend is left, one region at a time.
  7. Resonance Q last: it no longer moves anything above.

Cutoffs are estimated at full signal level with the same method as the VSID graph (within about 15 % of a measurement). Low in the curve the 6581 cutoff also depends on the signal level. PAL clock.
